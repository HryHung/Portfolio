05 / ENGINEERING CASE STUDY

Hexapod Robot.

Modular Walking Platform

OVERVIEW

A six-legged robotics platform for exploring mechanical design, inverse kinematics, gait generation, and embedded control. Three actuated joints per leg create an 18-DOF structure for experimentation with mechanisms, electrical interfaces, and future sensing equipment.

DESIGN / BUILD / EVALUATE

Engineering Approach

The project connects university robotics concepts with a physical platform. Mechanical assembly, firmware, and simulation form separate but related parts of the work; simulated motion is not presented as measured walking performance.

SYSTEM WORKFLOW

  1. 01Gait planning
  2. 02Inverse kinematics
  3. 03Servo control
  4. 04Leg actuation
01

Six legs built around modular interfaces

The body and leg assemblies were modeled in SolidWorks. Each leg contains coxa, femur, and tibia joints, giving three actuated degrees of freedom for foot positioning. Modular joint and leg interfaces are intended to simplify assembly and replacement during development.

The architecture combines available electronics modules with custom PCB work. Power contacts and alignment pins provide the mechanical–electrical interface, while magnetic features assist positioning. The magnets are not described as current-carrying contacts.

02

From a desired foot position to joint commands

MATLAB modeling investigates the relationship between the desired foot position and joint angles. Inverse kinematics provides that transformation, allowing gait development to be expressed in terms of foot trajectories rather than independent servo settings.

The software architecture separates high-level commands, gait planning, inverse kinematics, servo control, and hardware drivers. The gait model provides a way to examine coordinated movement before drawing conclusions about the behavior of the assembled mechanism.

03

Embedded integration & actuator feedback

Embedded C firmware on ESP32 implements low-level I²C driver integration and a servo-control layer. The central controller and electrical architecture connect the 18 actuators to the planned motion pipeline.

Early physical use exposed an actuator reliability issue: the project note records plastic MG996 servo gearboxes stripping after approximately four to five runs. That observation informs actuator selection and illustrates why successful simulation alone cannot establish hardware reliability. Terrain-aware walking, IMU/force feedback, advanced gait adaptation, and a possible ROS interface remain future work.

EVIDENCE & OUTCOMES

Results & Achievements

  • Physical six-legged, 18-DOF robot assembled.
  • Mechanical CAD, an electrical architecture, and gait/kinematics simulation developed.
  • Modular platform established for continued robotics experimentation.
  • Early gearbox failure documented after approximately four to five runs.

Evidence limits

The supplied evidence does not establish walking speed, payload, endurance, or measured gait stability. Simulation, physical assembly, and future sensor-feedback work are kept distinct.

Source basis: Hexapod project note; CV; portfolio page 5, qualified by the supplied evidence summary.
