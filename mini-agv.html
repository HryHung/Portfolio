06 / ENGINEERING CASE STUDY

Mini AGV.

undefined

OVERVIEW

A compact guided vehicle that transports manually loaded goods to destinations selected by cargo color. It follows a black line on a white surface, recognizes loading and branching locations, and routes red or blue cargo through an embedded mission sequence.

DESIGN / BUILD / EVALUATE

Engineering Approach

The project combines a fabricated chassis, custom electronics, calibrated sensing, mathematical modeling, and feedback control. The 0.5 m/s design maximum is separate from the reported 0.35–0.40 m/s operating range.

SYSTEM WORKFLOW

01

Integrating the chassis, drivetrain & electronics

A differential-drive layout uses two powered wheels and a passive front support. Drivetrain sizing, center-of-gravity calculations, structural deformation, and assembly tolerances were considered as parts of the same mechanical design.

CAD and structural simulation supported chassis development. Custom electronics connect the ESP32, sensor inputs, and motor actuation. The completed robot brings these components together in a compact physical prototype; the structural plot remains a simulation result rather than a measured payload test.

02

Calibrated sensing & nested control loops

Five TCRT5000 infrared sensors read the guide line. Their analog outputs are calibrated before a weighted-average method estimates line position. A TCS34725 color sensor uses calibrated thresholds to distinguish cargo colors.

PWM–encoder response data were collected for motor identification. First-order models fitted in MATLAB achieved 96.19% and 96.35% agreement with the identification data. An outer PD controller converts line-tracking error into wheel-speed demands; inner PI controllers regulate the individual wheels. MATLAB/Simulink tuning produced simulated settling times of approximately 0.15 seconds and overshoots of 1.87% and 1.92%. These are model results, not measured whole-vehicle settling times.

03

Mission sequencing & physical evaluation

A state machine coordinates line following, waiting for cargo, color processing, and decisions at junctions. Color recognition supplies a classification; the higher-level mission logic decides which route to follow. The web interface displays feedback during testing.

Evaluation combined observed route execution with a physical pen trace and the system’s estimated tracking error. One check measured approximately 2.5 mm beyond the line edge. However, the report’s conclusions disagree about satisfying the ±3 mm requirement, and its center-to-edge conversion uses a 12 mm half-width despite specifying a 26 mm line elsewhere. The observed result is retained without claiming consistently proven tracking accuracy.

EVIDENCE & OUTCOMES

Results & Achievements

  • Loading-stop detection, red/blue cargo recognition, and branch selection demonstrated.
  • Reported average operating speed: 0.35–0.40 m/s; endpoint stopping error: <5 mm.
  • Motor-model fits: 96.19% and 96.35% against identification data.
  • Simulated PI wheel-control response: approximately 0.15 s settling; 1.87% and 1.92% overshoot.
  • One physical pen-trace check: approximately 2.5 mm beyond the line edge.

Evidence limits

Payload and maximum speed remain design specifications. Conflicting tracking conclusions and the line-width inconsistency prevent a claim of consistently verified ±3 mm accuracy. The report also notes sensor noise, PCB corrections, and further control tuning needs.

Source basis: Mini AGV report, sensing/control and experimental chapters; portfolio page 6; supplied project summary.
