03 / ENGINEERING CASE STUDY

Autonomous Mobile Robot.

Indoor Warehouse Transportation

OVERVIEW

An indoor transportation robot connecting warehouse tasks with mapping, navigation, and visual docking. Mechanical design, electrical architecture, embedded motor control, and ROS 2 come together in a working prototype operated through a browser interface.

DESIGN / BUILD / EVALUATE

Engineering Approach

The work covers a full-scale engineering design and a lower-power physical prototype. The prototype validates control integration and operating principles; it does not validate every payload, speed, or endurance requirement of the full design.

01

Requirements, drivetrain & structural design

Payload, footprint, speed, operating time, and docking requirements set the design envelope. Weighted Pugh matrices compared drivetrain, sensing, power, and controller options before detailed component selection.

A differential-drive arrangement with centrally positioned drive wheels was selected. The mechanical work connected torque and transmission calculations to shaft dimensions, bearing selection, load stability, and braking conditions. CAD mass-property analysis informed packaging; finite-element analysis assessed critical drivetrain components. These calculations and simulations support the design, rather than proving full-load prototype capability.

02

Power distribution & embedded motion control

The electrical architecture separates propulsion and control power distribution, with battery, motor-driver, sensor, and charging arrangements considered together. This makes the power system part of the overall robot design rather than an isolated electronics task.

Raspberry Pi handles higher-level processing, while ESP32 acquires encoder data and executes wheel-speed control. Differential-drive kinematics translate robot motion commands into wheel commands, with PI/PID feedback at the embedded layer. The physical build tests communication and actuation across these layers.

03

Mapping, waypoint navigation & visual docking

SLAM Toolbox builds the map while an operator manually drives the robot, with a forward-obstacle check. AMCL then localizes the robot on a saved map, and Nav2 executes a configured waypoint sequence. The Flask interface brings manual control, map creation, mission configuration, and execution status into one operating workflow.

For final approach, the camera was calibrated using 50 checkerboard images. AprilTag observations support approach correction and expose distance and alignment feedback in the interface. The docking stage is distinct from Nav2 waypoint arrival: the documented navigation-only test did not establish precise final docking.

04

What the prototype tests establish

Physical mapping and waypoint missions demonstrated that the operator interface, ROS 2 software, and embedded control could work together. Map dimensions were compared with rounded reference measurements obtained using an iPhone measuring application; that method also contributes uncertainty.

The path analysis compares onboard localization with the Nav2 reference path over 297 moving samples. It measures agreement within the robot’s own navigation system, not independently surveyed positioning accuracy. Wheel-speed feedback remained inconsistent, and continuous camera streaming sometimes overloaded the Raspberry Pi.

EVIDENCE & OUTCOMES

Results & Achievements

  • Physical mapping, waypoint execution, and web status updates demonstrated.
  • Room-map differences: 0.16 m in width (2.11%) and 0.10 m in length (1.04%) against the report’s reference measurements.
  • One AprilTag docking case: 0.500 m target, 0.515 m camera-reported distance — 15 mm distance error.
  • Onboard estimated path deviation: 16.5 mm mean and 159.7 mm maximum across 297 moving samples.

Evidence limits

The 20 kg payload, 1 m/s maximum speed, and ≥4-hour runtime are design targets, not fully validated prototype capabilities. The docking case does not establish repeatability or completed autonomous charging. Path deviation is not an independent ground-truth measurement.
