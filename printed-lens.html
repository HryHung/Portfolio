07 / ENGINEERING CASE STUDY

3D Printed Lens.

Film Optics Adapted for Digital Photography

OVERVIEW

A customer-driven product developed during the Technix AM internship to reuse the lens and viewfinder from a sentimental film camera. A custom printed housing, manual focusing mechanism, camera interface, and hot-shoe accessory adapt those recovered components for digital photography.

DESIGN / BUILD / EVALUATE

Engineering Approach

The objective was to preserve usable optical elements while creating a practical mechanical interface for a modern camera. The work combines reverse engineering and optical requirements with iterative CAD, printing, fitting, and image-capture tests.

01

Recovering components & defining the optical fit

Development began with the customer’s requirements and the recovered camera components. Disassembly and measurement established the dimensions needed to retain the original optics and viewfinder while designing new surrounding parts.

The optical–mechanical task was to understand focusing and the lens-to-sensor spacing required to form an image. The new housing needed to locate the recovered elements, provide a compatible camera interface, and allow deliberate focus adjustment. The optical reference illustrates the principle; it is not a prescription or measured performance model for this particular lens.

02

Refining the focusing mechanism through printed prototypes

The mechanism began with a helical focusing approach, then was refined into a telescoping arrangement with guidance to constrain unwanted rotation. This development connected the intended focus motion with the physical fit and compactness of the assembly.

SolidWorks was used to design the parts, with Cura and PETG printing supporting fabrication. Fit and printing tolerances were considered through iterative fitting, along with the intended outdoor use. Prototype feedback informed revisions to optical-element retention, focusing operation, and the camera mount. The evidence describes development iterations rather than a quantified durability test.

03

Camera testing & customer delivery

The finished assembly was checked on a Sony digital camera for mount compatibility, focusing, and image capture. The supplied result photograph shows a Sony ZV-E10 displaying an image, providing direct evidence that the adapted optics and printed mechanism function together.

A separate hot-shoe viewfinder accessory also received customer approval. The supplied internship-report summary describes a finished product satisfactory to the customer and further company investment in development and preparation for repeat production. It does not establish a production volume, sales result, or measured optical quality.

EVIDENCE & OUTCOMES

Results & Achievements

  • Functioning manual-focus lens completed using recovered film-camera optics.
  • Sony camera compatibility and image capture demonstrated.
  • Separate hot-shoe viewfinder accessory approved by the customer.
  • Finished product reported as satisfactory; further product development initiated by the company.

Evidence limits

No quantitative resolution, distortion, durability, sales, or production-volume results are supplied. The evidence supports a functioning customer-oriented prototype and product-development outcome.
